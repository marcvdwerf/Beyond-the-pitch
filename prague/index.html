One country's story.

You do not need to memorise dates. The route is designed to make the history feel clear, vivid and accessible.

01

National Theatre Courtyard

A nation is born - and a sport starts to matter

In 1918, Czechoslovakia becomes independent after centuries of Habsburg rule. Within a generation, ice hockey moves from pastime to national symbol. This is where Kamil explains why a sport can become part of how a country sees itself - and what it means when that symbol is later controlled, threatened or taken away.

02

Žofín River Island

The first rink - and a match that carried political weight

Prague's original hockey stadium stood here. In the late 1930s, hockey was already entangled with a Europe sliding into fear and confrontation. Kamil uses this stop to show how public sport can become a stage for something much bigger than entertainment - especially when a country knows what may be coming next.

03

Zlatá Hospůdka

When the state turned on its own champions

In 1950, members of the national hockey team - medalists, champions, public figures - were arrested by the communist regime and sentenced to prison and forced labour. This is one of the heaviest moments in the route, and one of the clearest examples of what happens when power decides that even sport must submit.

This is the emotional centre of the walk: the point where hockey stops looking like a pastime and starts looking like a public language the regime wanted to control.
04

Myslíkova Street

When sport became performance for the state

Under communism, public movement, mass display and organised sport became propaganda tools as well as leisure. At this stop, Kamil connects personal stories to national choreography: how bodies, crowds and ritual were used to express obedience, spectacle and social order.

05

Lucerna Palace

A Prague what-if - and a breath in the middle of the route

Lucerna carries one of those details visitors rarely hear: it was once imagined as something closer to a hockey arena than the cultural venue people know today. It offers a lighter, more local kind of insight - the kind of stop that reminds you this route is a conversation, not a lecture.

06

Wenceslas Square

Tanks, then goals - and what a victory can unlock

After the Soviet-led invasion of 1968, hockey became charged in a way outsiders can easily miss. Victories over the USSR were not experienced as simple sporting results. Here, Kamil shows how a match can absorb anger, hope and public feeling - and how quickly celebration can turn political.

07

Politických Vězňů Street

The Velvet Revolution - and the end of one shared chapter

The late 1980s reshape the country. Kamil explains how the Velvet Revolution changed public life, and how the later split of Czechoslovakia created two nations, two identities and two hockey stories. By now, the route has moved from control and fear toward self-definition and memory.

08

Czech Hockey Hall of Fame

Nagano, memory and the moments that still live

The route ends where pride becomes tangible. The Hall of Fame brings together the players, trophies, footage and objects that carried Czech hockey into public memory - including the moments that made the country stop, look up and believe again. By this point, the exhibits make sense in a deeper way.

Frequently asked questions

Good to know

Do I need to know anything about ice hockey before I join? +

Nothing at all. Most people who join this tour have never watched a hockey match in their lives. That's fine - the sport makes sense once you understand the context. Kamil builds the whole thing from the ground up, and the historical thread works even if you've never followed hockey.

When is the hockey season? +

The Czech Extraliga runs September through April. Match tickets for Sparta Praha or HC Slavia Praha are included in the Version B price when a suitable fixture is available for your dates. Outside of season, Version A - the walking tour and Hall of Fame - runs year-round and is a complete experience in its own right.

How does the booking process work? +

Use the enquiry form below or email us directly. Tell us your preferred date, group size, and which version interests you. We review each request personally within 24- 48 hours, confirm Kamil's availability and match access where relevant, then send you next steps to finalise. We don't take bookings on autopilot - everything is checked before it's confirmed.

What if no match is available for my dates? +

We'll tell you clearly, suggest the closest alternative dates if they work for you, or offer Version A - the walking tour and Hall of Fame - as a fully independent option. You won't be pushed into something unsuitable. If none of the alternatives work, there's no charge.

Is the history heavy? Is it appropriate for all ages? +

The history is real and Kamil doesn't sanitise it. The Nazi occupation and communist arrests are central to the story. He presents it with honesty and human perspective, not shock. Teens and adults will find it compelling. Younger children are welcome with some parental context around the heavier moments.

How is this different from a regular Prague walking tour? +

Most Prague tours take you to the same five landmarks and call it history. This one uses sport as the thread - and sport turns out to be the most honest way to understand a culture. You'll stand on Wenceslas Square and actually understand what happened there. That's a different kind of tour.

How many people can join? +

Groups from 2 people up to around 25. We do not accept groups of 30 or more - the format doesn't work at that scale. If you're planning a larger group trip to Prague, get in touch and we can discuss what's realistic.

What if I'm visiting Prague as part of a longer Europe trip? +

Prague is one of Europe's most accessible cities - direct trains from Berlin, Vienna and Munich; direct flights from London, Amsterdam, Paris and most major hubs. The tour works perfectly as a 2- 3 hour block within a Europe trip. If you can be in Prague for a weekend, you have more than enough time for this and everything else the city offers.

Can I book for a solo traveller? +

The minimum group size is 2 people. If you're travelling solo, get in touch anyway - occasionally we can match solo travellers with other small groups on the same date. We'll let you know what's possible.
